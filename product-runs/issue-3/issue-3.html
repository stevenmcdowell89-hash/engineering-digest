Trend · Distribution
The offer follows the wallet

The card-linked offer has always had an assumed home — the bank app. This month it turned up somewhere else three times: inside a payments platform, in an open invitation to build a wallet, and on an airline's card where the banks are reduced to the issuing rail.

A competitor's platform ships inside a payments app

Rezolve Ai embedded Reward's customer-engagement and commerce-media platform into Zilch on 21 July, putting personalised offers from hundreds of UK retailers directly into the payments journey of a fintech with nearly six million customers and more than $3.3bn a year flowing to partner merchants. Reward — acquired by Rezolve in February — runs in more than fifteen markets across the UK, Europe, the Middle East and Asia and has returned over $2bn in cashback to date. Its distribution has been banks; this is the same stack arriving through a payments app instead.

Source: Rezolve Ai (GlobeNewswire)

Mastercard opens wallet-building to anyone — and names rewards as the reason

Wallet Services, launched on 15 July, gives banks, fintechs, merchants and digital platforms the software kits, secure-element applet and tokenisation needed to put contactless payments inside their own iOS and Android apps, without separate operating-system integrations or their own EMVCo certification. Coverage spans the 200-plus countries where Mastercard's tokenisation service runs, iOS first in the US, UK and Europe. Chief digital officer Pablo Fourez put the point in loyalty terms: new wallets let companies "offer new benefits, rewards, discounts, points or features to encourage users to start using their wallets."

Source: PYMNTS · Fintech News Singapore

An airline takes the programme; the banks take the rail (Saudi Arabia)

Riyadh Air launched RX Pay in late July — a suite of Mastercard-branded cards issued by Saudi banks, but carrying the airline's own Sfeer Points as the earn currency on everyday spending, and newly redeemable against any Mastercard purchase globally rather than only against flights. Multi-currency, split payments and instalments come as standard, with a phased rollout from Saudi Arabia under an exclusivity with Mastercard. In the reader's Gulf region, the loyalty brand belongs to the airline and the bank is the plumbing.

Source: Riyadh Air (via Zawya) · Gulf Business

What it looks like in product →

The category's distribution assumption is that a card-linked offer reaches a customer because a bank puts it in front of them. Two things this month sit outside that. Reward's platform is live in a payments app, not a bank app, reaching six million people through a fintech's own journey. And Mastercard has made the contactless credential something any app can hold, with the network itself describing rewards, discounts and points as the reason a company would build one. Neither is a forecast; both shipped in July.

Sources: Rezolve Ai · Mastercard via PYMNTS

Trend · Agentic commerce
The agent rails go live at the banks

In June the networks specified who an agent may pay; last month a commerce platform opened the checkout to every merchant. This month the reader's own bank customers started transacting — and the hook that would let an agent see a loyalty account turned out to be identity, switched on almost nowhere.

Every major UK banking group, live

On 3 July Visa announced that AI agents had completed live purchases in Europe on consumers' behalf, browsing, selecting and buying within user-defined parameters. More than thirty banks took part, among them Barclays, HSBC UK, Lloyds Banking Group, Nationwide, NatWest, Revolut and Klarna, with lastminute.com, Frasers, Cleverbridge and BrickDepot on the merchant side and Visa Payment Passkeys, the Trusted Agent Protocol and an Agent Directory underneath. The announcement covers authentication, identity and settlement. It names no offer, no reward and no points balance.

Source: FStech

Identity becomes the layer everything else hangs off

The capability that lets an agent see a shopper's member pricing and loyalty benefits is identity linking, added to the Universal Commerce Protocol in March. In July the protocol's technical council decided to carry saved payment methods on that same identity-linking infrastructure rather than build a separate extension — making identity the foundation for transacting at all. Adoption tells the other half of the story. The protocol tracker UCP Checker counts identity capabilities live at fifteen stores, three further vendors declaring identity-flavoured extensions, 99.7% of verified stores running a single spec revision — and zero of 11,414 verified stores exposing payment capabilities at all.

Source: UCP Checker, July state report · Google (identity linking, 19 March)

Nothing shipped on the incentive side

Across the same period an independent protocol tracker records no prominent loyalty-programme integrations and no dedicated discount protocols announced anywhere in the agentic stack. The two standards that would carry incentives both predate the window: Talon.One's Unified Incentives Protocol (28 January) and the identity-linking capability itself (19 March). What moved in July was payment authorisation, signature algorithms and 3-D Secure — the plumbing for a transaction, not for what makes one worth doing.

Source: agenticplug protocol tracker · Talon.One (UIP, 28 January)

What it looks like in product →

Two issues ago the gap was that the agent rails had no loyalty object. It is more specific now. The object exists and it rides on identity linking — the same capability the protocol's technical council has just made foundational to transacting — and it is live at fifteen stores against more than eleven thousand verified, none of them yet exposing payment. The rails reached the UK's banks this month; the thing that would let an agent see a customer's rewards reached fifteen shops.

Sources: FStech on Visa · UCP Checker · agenticplug

Trend · Competitive set
Everyone is selling the engagement layer

Within three weeks a card network, a marketing-automation platform and a loyalty specialist each pitched the same product — personalised engagement, sold as a service — into the same bank and retailer budgets. The competitive set for the engagement layer is no longer other loyalty vendors alone.

The network sells engagement

On its 28 July quarterly call Visa described value-added services — up 34% to $3.8bn and now close to a third of its revenue — as increasingly a loyalty and engagement engine. The examples were campaign-level: a Brazilian bank's World Cup promotion drew a million participants, an 8% lift in card activation and $400m of incremental payment volume, while a Mexican co-brand saw average transaction size rise fivefold on match days (Brazil and Mexico). Visa Direct now powers DoorDash's Crimson rewards-and-banking programme, and a white-label AI financial assistant is being rolled out for banks to put inside their own apps.

Source: The Wise Marketer

The marketing platform sells loyalty

Dotdigital launched a native loyalty product on 16 July, alongside research across 4,000 consumers in the UK, US, Australia and Singapore. Points-based programmes came top at 62%, ahead of free shipping at 53% and cashback at 52%; 45% said they had raised their spending to reach the next tier and 53% called loyalty programmes important to their future relationship with a brand. The number the launch was built on: only 15% find the marketing they receive "very relevant".

Source: Dotdigital (PR Newswire)

The specialist sells scale (Vietnam)

Eagle Eye's AI-powered programme for Central Retail Vietnam, The 1 Vietnam, passed 5.8 million members within months of launch — reported in the trade press on 27 July. A large retailer in the reader's Asian footprint bought the engagement layer rather than building it, and got to a membership base of that size quickly enough for the number itself to be the story.

Source: The Wise Marketer

Why it matters

Three sellers, three different routes to the same buyer. A network bundles engagement into services it already sells issuers; a marketing-automation platform extends into loyalty from the campaign side; a specialist wins on delivered scale. Each arrives with an existing relationship the others lack — the network with the issuer, the platform with the marketing team, the specialist with the retailer — and the consumer research landing the same month says the deciding weakness is relevance, not reward value.
